Buying vs building internal tools

A clear-eyed way to decide where custom software genuinely pays off — and where it just adds burden.

SA Sam Ayodele7 min readVatous Insights

Sooner or later, every growing organization faces the question: should we buy this software off the shelf, or build something of our own? Get it right and you gain leverage. Get it wrong and you inherit either a straightjacket or a maintenance burden.

Buy when the problem is common

If your need is well understood and shared by thousands of other businesses — email, accounting, payroll — buy. A mature product will almost always beat what you could build, and someone else carries the upkeep.

Build when the process is your edge

Custom software earns its keep when the workflow is specific to how you win — the thing that makes you faster or better than competitors. Forcing that into a generic tool often means bending your business to the software instead of the reverse.

  • Is this process a genuine differentiator?
  • Do off-the-shelf tools force painful compromises?
  • Can we maintain what we build?
  • What is the real total cost over three years?
Build what makes you different. Buy what makes you the same.

The hybrid reality

Most good architectures are a blend: buy the commodities, build the differentiators, and integrate them cleanly so data flows between the two. The skill is in drawing that line deliberately rather than by accident.

Decide with eyes open, count the full cost, and make sure whatever you build, you can also run.
